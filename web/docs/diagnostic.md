# Diagnostic

The hardest part of mixed-level DSA study is *not* learning the material — it's deciding where to spend the hour you have today. This diagnostic answers that question with a weakness vector across the twelve topic clusters that make up the interview surface.

Why sixty questions? Each of the twelve clusters gets five — enough to tell "shaky recognition" apart from "memorised, understood, drilled." Fewer, and the signal is noisy; more, and nobody finishes. Fifteen minutes is the ceiling for an *honest* diagnostic — any longer and you start fatiguing into mistakes that over-weight your weaknesses.

## How to read your result

Three bands by score:

- **WEAK (< 60 %)** — you can't rely on reflex. Open the chapter and work the canonical problem list top to bottom. These are the clusters that will cost you a round.
- **OK (60–79 %)** — recognition is there; execution isn't automatic. Skim the chapter's templates and the trap callouts; drill 3–5 problems to turn knowledge into reflex.
- **STRONG (≥ 80 %)** — you can skip the chapter for now. Spend the time instead on hard variants (the "Related problems" at the bottom of each pattern page) and on mocks.

The study order the diagnostic produces is strict-weakest-first *with one override*: before starting the top cluster, open the [prerequisite map](/map) and check its ancestors. If any ancestor is also WEAK or OK, start there instead — otherwise the top-cluster chapter will keep tripping you on prereq material instead of its own.

## What this diagnostic will NOT tell you

- **Whether you can code the solution under pressure** — it only tests the recognition and reasoning layer. Pattern recognition is the first bottleneck; implementation fluency is the second. Mock interviews fill the second gap.
- **System-design readiness** — a separate axis entirely. See the [System Design](/system-design/) chapter.
- **Behavioural / communication skills** — see [behavioural crash course](/appendix/behavioral-crash-course).

A good score here means your map of the surface is correct. The ground-truth signal is still "can I solve a cold problem in 30 minutes, aloud, correctly." Use this to find the right chapters to open; use mocks to prove you actually own them.
