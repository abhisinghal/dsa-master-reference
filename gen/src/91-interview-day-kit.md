# Interview Day Kit

*The 24 hours before your interview matter more than the previous 3 weeks combined. Use this as a checklist.*

<StreakTracker />

## The night before

- [ ] **Sleep** — 8+ hours. No exceptions. Cognitive load in interviews is brutal.
- [ ] **Skim the [Traps Catalog](/appendix/traps-catalog)** — 15 min max. Just refresh recognition; don't re-derive.
- [ ] **Skim the [Cheat Sheets](/appendix/cheatsheets)** — pattern templates + Java gotchas.
- [ ] **Prepare 2 [Behavioral stories](/foundations/playbook)** — one Ownership, one Failure.
- [ ] **No new problems tonight** — refreshing is fine; learning new material adds stress.

## Morning of

- [ ] **Coffee ready 60 min before interview** — caffeine peaks in ~45 min.
- [ ] **Water, snack, bathroom break** — done before interview starts.
- [ ] **Test video/mic** — join call 5 min early to check setup.
- [ ] **Quiet room** — headphones on, phone silent, tabs closed.
- [ ] **Notepad + pen ready** — for hand-tracing.

## In the interview

- [ ] **Clarify first** — 2 minutes minimum. Get the input types, size, edge cases pinned down.
- [ ] **State the pattern out loud** — "This looks like Sliding Window because…"
- [ ] **Complexity BEFORE code** — say "target O(n)" and explain why.
- [ ] **Handle edge cases proactively** — empty, single, adversarial.
- [ ] **Test with example** — hand-trace at the end.
- [ ] **Ask a clarifying question if stuck** — better than silence.

## Common day-of mistakes

<Callout kind="trap" title="Overthinking after they say 'looks good'">
When the interviewer nods and says "looks good", stop refactoring. Move to the next thing. Perfection isn't the goal.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="trap" title="Coding before agreeing on approach">
"Let me start coding and see" is a red flag. Get buy-in on the algorithm first, THEN code. 3 minutes of clarification saves 15 minutes of rework.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="trap" title="Not testing your code">
Interviewers don't want to compile it — they want to see YOU trace it. Walk through Example 1 out loud after finishing.
</Callout>

## Emergency mode

**If you're stuck for 5+ minutes and interviewer isn't nudging:**

1. Say: "Let me talk through what I'm thinking so you can spot where I'm off."
2. State the brute force out loud. Get partial credit.
3. Ask: "Would a hint be OK, or should I keep going?"

**If you can't solve it in the time:**

1. Explain the brute force + why you can't optimize.
2. Ask: "Is there a specific pattern you'd like me to try?"
3. Don't apologize excessively. Move on with dignity.
